# HVAC market research notes

Date: 2026-10-12. Purpose: ground the HVAC-focused rewrite of the site in what HVAC owners care about and what rules apply.
Reliability note: most numeric claims below come from vendor blogs or press coverage of paid reports. They are **directional only** and are deliberately **not** published on the site as fact.

## 1. Competitive landscape
- Many vendors sell AI receptionists to home-service trades (plumbing, HVAC, electrical), typically advertising 24/7 answering, emergency detection, and booking into ServiceTitan (via API) or Jobber/ServiceTitan (via Zapier). Directories already list ~25 such tools.
- Implication: "AI receptionist" alone is a crowded, commoditised pitch. Our differentiation is **HVAC-only depth + a done-for-you front office** (calls, lead follow-up, maintenance agreements, reviews, back office), emergency safety rules (gas smell / CO escalates to a person), and seasonal playbooks (tune-up campaigns, peak-season overflow).
- Verify any vendor's integration status before we claim a specific platform.

## 2. What HVAC owners say they struggle with
- EGIA 2026 HVAC Contractor Survey: top external threats are labor shortages, rising costs and regulation; top internal challenges are hiring/retention, lead generation and operations management. Nearly all surveyed companies raised prices in the past year.
- ServiceTitan 2026 Residential State of the Trades (press coverage): labor and overhead costs are the top concern; more contractors are turning to customer retention and AI to offset costs.
- ACHR News: about a quarter of residential contractors report using AI to boost business.
- Implication: lead with **"add capacity without hiring"** and **retention** (maintenance agreements), not generic "automation".

## 3. Speed to lead / missed calls
- Direction is consistent across sources: the first company to respond wins more jobs; unanswered calls rarely leave voicemails.
- Specific figures (e.g. 73% vs 4% booking by response time, $1,200 per missed call, 27–38% after-hours misses) trace back to vendor material with no primary study found.
- Implication: use an **interactive calculator** where the visitor enters their own call volume and job value instead of quoting statistics.

## 4. Maintenance agreements
- Typical residential plans are roughly $150–$500 per year; renewal benchmarks quoted range 70–90% (vendor sources). Agreements drive repeat and add-on work.
- Implication: maintenance-agreement renewals and tune-up campaigns are a core offer, framed around recurring revenue.

## 5. Compliance (must be designed in, not bolted on)
- **TCPA and AI voices:** FCC Declaratory Ruling (Feb 8, 2024) treats AI-generated voices as "artificial" voices under the TCPA, so the consent rules for artificial/prerecorded voice calls apply. Practical rule for us: AI **answers inbound** calls; **outbound** AI calls/texts go only to people who have contacted the business or opted in, with consent recorded.
- **Call recording:** Federal law is one-party consent, but a group of states requires all-party consent (commonly listed: CA, CT, DE, FL, IL, MD, MA, MI, MT, NV, NH, PA, WA; lists vary, and some have exceptions). Safe design: **announce recording at the start of every call.**
- Implication: a visible, plain-language "Built for trust" section; a compliance review step in the audit; copy that never promises legal compliance. Counsel should review.

## 6. Messaging and tone decisions
- Speak in owner language: calls, trucks, techs, comfort advisors, tune-ups, maintenance agreements, peak season, no-cool / no-heat.
- Use the "cool vs heat" visual language (blue and amber) instead of generic AI visuals.
- No invented statistics, logos, testimonials or case-study results. Playbooks (designs) replace placeholder case studies until real results exist.
